When someone gives you an objection:

The POTENT Rule

Never defend. Never argue. Discover.

When someone pushes back, do not immediately explain. Do not become emotional. Do not try to prove them wrong. Become curious.

The 3 A Framework

1. Acknowledge

Show the customer you heard them: “That makes sense.” “I understand.” “I appreciate you sharing that.”

2. Associate

Connect their concern to a bigger conversation: “I hear that from many companies we speak with. Most already have transportation options in place. The reason they look for another partner is usually when their normal option cannot cover an urgent situation.”

3. Ask

Return control through a question: “What specifically makes you feel that way?” “What would need to happen for this to make sense?” “What is the biggest concern preventing you from moving forward?”

The Opening, Discovery & The 1–10 Question

Source — Master Sales Book, Chapter 7 — “The POTENT Calling & Conversation System”. Original wording, unchanged.

The POTENT Opening

Don't sound like a telemarketer. Don't rush. Don't read the script like you're trying to finish it.

“Hey, this is [NAME] with POTENT Operations. How are you doing today?”

Let them answer. Then:

“I'll keep this quick—I know you've got a business to run. Did I catch you with about 30 seconds?”

That's Southern hospitality. You're respecting their time. If yes:

“I appreciate it. The reason I'm calling is pretty simple. We help businesses when they have freight that needs to move quickly, when their regular transportation can't cover something, or when they need another reliable option. Out of curiosity, how are you currently handling those situations?”

Then STOP. Listen.

Why We Ask “Out Of Curiosity”

Because we're genuinely trying to understand.

  • “Out of curiosity, how often does that happen?”
  • “Out of curiosity, what normally happens when your regular carrier can't cover it?”
  • “Out of curiosity, what's the biggest headache with that process?”

Questions make people think. And your Playbook principle remains:

Questions are more valuable than answers.

The 1–10 Question

Once you've learned about their current system:

“Just so I understand where you're at, on a scale of 1–10, how satisfied are you with the way you're handling urgent transportation right now?”

If they say 10: “That's great. What makes it a 10?” Learn.

If they say 7: “Got you. Why isn't it a 10?” That's where the conversation gets interesting.

Never tell them why they're wrong. Let them tell you.

Digging Deeper

When they identify a problem:

  • “How long has that been an issue?”
  • “What have you tried so far?”
  • “Why hasn't that completely solved it?”
  • “What happens when that occurs?”
  • “How does that affect your operation?”
  • “How important is it for you to fix that?”

Then: “On a scale of 1–10, how important is it?” If they say 6: “Why isn't it lower?”

Don't ask: “Why isn't it higher?”

Ask: “Why isn't it lower?”

They will sell themselves on the importance of solving their own problem.

Current State → Desired State

Ask: “Where are you now?” Understand it. Then: “Where would you like to be instead?” Then: “What's keeping you from getting there?”

Now you have the gap. That's what POTENT sells into.

Not a truck. Not a mile. Not a transportation service. A solution to a problem they already told you they have.

If the phone rings while you're doing something else — that's the business. Don't make the customer feel like they're bothering you. Answer with energy.

“POTENT Operations, this is [NAME]. How can I help you?”

Then listen. Don't immediately interrupt with “What's the pickup?” First understand what they need.

Cold-Call, Email, Voicemail & LinkedIn Scripts

Source — Master Sales Book, Chapter 7 (First Contact & Cold Outreach) and Chapter 16 (The Sales Conversation System). Every script is word for word.

The Goal Of The First Contact

The biggest mistake in cold outreach is trying to sell immediately. A cold call is not where you close a deal. A cold call is where you earn the next conversation.

The objective: start a relationship. Find the right person. Discover a problem.

The POTENT First Contact Mindset

You are not interrupting. You are not begging for business. You are introducing a potential solution.

Businesses have problems every day. Your job is to find out if one of those problems exists.

The First 10 Seconds

People decide quickly if they want to continue listening.

Do not start with: “Hi, I'm calling from POTENT OPERATIONS and we offer...”

Everyone sounds the same. Instead: be direct. Be professional. Create curiosity.

Cold Call Framework

Step 1 — Permission

“Hi, this is [Name] with POTENT OPERATIONS. Did I catch you with a quick minute?”

Why this works: it shows respect. It lowers resistance.

Step 2 — Reason For Calling

“I'll be brief. We work with companies that need reliable transportation support when normal options cannot cover urgent situations.”

Step 3 — Ask A Question

“I'm curious, how does your company currently handle emergency or time-sensitive transportation needs?”

Then listen.

The Complete Freight Introduction Script

Prospect: “Hello.”

“Hi, this is [Name] with POTENT OPERATIONS. Did I catch you with a quick minute?”

(Wait.)

“Great. The reason I'm reaching out is we work with businesses that need reliable transportation support for urgent or time-sensitive shipments. I was curious, how does your team currently handle situations when a shipment needs to move quickly and your normal carrier isn't available?”

STOP TALKING. Let them answer.

If They Say:

“We already have carriers.”

Response: “That makes sense. Most companies we speak with already have transportation providers. I'm not looking to replace anyone. I'm curious, what happens when those carriers are at capacity or cannot cover an urgent request?”

“We don't need anyone right now.”

Response: “I understand. Most companies don't need emergency support every day. The reason I reached out is because these situations usually happen when nobody expects them. Do you currently have a backup option in place if something urgent comes up?”

“Send me information.”

Response: “Absolutely, I can send that over. So I make sure I send something relevant, can I ask what transportation challenges your team deals with most often?”

Gatekeeper Strategy

Gatekeepers are not obstacles. They are people. Treat them professionally. Their job is protecting someone's time. Your job is earning trust.

Do NOT say: “I need to speak to whoever is in charge.”

That creates resistance.

Say: “I was hoping you could point me in the right direction. Who handles transportation decisions for your company?”

If they ask, “What is this regarding?” say:

“We help companies with backup transportation and urgent delivery situations. I just want to make sure I connect with the person responsible for that area.”

The Follow-Up Rule

Most salespeople quit too early. Professional follow-up is not bothering someone. It is staying available.

The POTENT Standard

Every first interaction should leave the customer thinking, “That was different.” Not because we had the best pitch. Because we showed respect, curiosity, professionalism, and understanding.

Qualify Before You Chase

Not every lead deserves equal time. A salesperson needs to qualify opportunities.

The POTENT Qualification Framework

1. Need

Do they have a problem we solve?

“How often do urgent transportation situations happen?”

“What challenges do you experience with your current setup?”

2. Impact

Does the problem matter?

“What happens when that occurs?”

“Does that create delays or additional costs?”

3. Timing

When could this become important?

“Is this something you are currently dealing with?”

“Are there upcoming projects or changes where additional support would help?”

4. Authority

Can this person make decisions?

“Who else is involved when choosing transportation partners?”

“What does your approval process usually look like?”

5. Fit

Are we a good match?

“Are you looking for a reliable backup option, or are you mainly focused on finding the lowest possible price?”

The Close

After you've listened and confirmed the problem:

“Based on what you've told me, it sounds like [PROBLEM] is really the biggest issue. Did I get that right?”

If yes: “And what you're really looking for is [DESIRED RESULT], correct?” If yes: “Then I think we can help you with that. Let's get you set up.”

Stop talking. Let them respond.

The POTENT Closing Belief

The customer should never feel sold. They should feel understood.

The goal is not: “How do I get them to say yes?”

The goal is: “Have we discovered enough value that moving forward makes sense?”

Before You Close

Never rush to a quote. Before presenting a solution, confirm:

Problem — “From what you've shared, the biggest challenge is ___, correct?”

Impact — “And when that happens, it creates ___ for your team, correct?”

Goal — “So ideally, you would want a transportation partner that can ___, correct?”

This is called agreement stacking. The customer confirms their own situation.

The POTENT Solution Presentation

Do not dump information. Do not explain every feature. Connect the solution to their problem.

“Based on what you told me, the biggest issue is having coverage when your normal carriers cannot respond quickly. What we do is provide an additional transportation option for those situations so your team is not scrambling when something urgent happens.”

Never Sell The Truck

The truck is not the value. The value is:

  • Preventing delays
  • Protecting deadlines
  • Reducing stress
  • Giving options
  • Saving time

Closing Techniques

The Assumptive Close

Use when the customer is clearly interested.

“Based on what we discussed, the next step would be getting your company set up as a customer so we can be available when you need support. Does that make sense?”

The Choice Close

Give two options.

“Would it make more sense to start with emergency support only, or would you like to discuss becoming a regular backup transportation partner?”

The Next Step Close

Not every close is a contract. Sometimes the close is sending paperwork, scheduling a meeting, getting approved as a vendor, or completing a trial shipment.

“What would be the best next step from your side to get us approved as a transportation option?”

The Direct Close

Sometimes you simply ask.

“Based on everything we've discussed, is there any reason we shouldn't move forward?”

Then be quiet.

Silence Is A Skill

Many salespeople lose deals because they talk after asking for the business. They ask, “Does that work for you?” then immediately continue explaining.

Don't. Let the customer answer. Silence creates space for decisions.

Handling Last-Minute Hesitation

Customer: “I just need to think about it.”

Response: “Of course. Before you do, is there anything specific you're unsure about?”

Customer: “I need to talk to my partner/boss.”

Response: “That makes sense. What do you think they will want to know before making a decision?”

Customer: “Send me a proposal.”

Response: “Absolutely. Before I put that together, what are the most important things you would like the proposal to solve?”

Creating Urgency The Right Way

Never create fake urgency.

Do not say: “Sign today or the offer disappears.”

That is not our style. Real urgency comes from consequences.

“What happens if this situation occurs again and you don't have another option?”

“How important is it to have a backup plan before the next emergency?”

The POTENT Customer Onboarding Close

When a customer agrees, do not celebrate too early. The relationship is just beginning. Confirm:

  • Company information
  • Billing contact
  • Service expectations
  • Communication preferences
  • Emergency contact
  • Payment terms

Professional onboarding creates repeat business.

The Customer Commitment Question

A powerful final question:

“What would make you confident choosing POTENT as your transportation partner?”

The answer tells you exactly what matters most.

The Opening, Discovery & The 1–10 Question

The POTENT Opening

Don't sound like a telemarketer. Don't rush. Don't read the script like you're trying to finish it.

“Hey, this is [NAME] with POTENT Operations. How are you doing today?”

Let them answer. Then:

“I'll keep this quick—I know you've got a business to run. Did I catch you with about 30 seconds?”

That's Southern hospitality. You're respecting their time. If yes:

“I appreciate it. The reason I'm calling is pretty simple. We help businesses when they have freight that needs to move quickly, when their regular transportation can't cover something, or when they need another reliable option. Out of curiosity, how are you currently handling those situations?”

Then STOP. Listen.

Why We Ask “Out Of Curiosity”

Because we're genuinely trying to understand.

  • “Out of curiosity, how often does that happen?”
  • “Out of curiosity, what normally happens when your regular carrier can't cover it?”
  • “Out of curiosity, what's the biggest headache with that process?”

Questions make people think. And your Playbook principle remains:

Questions are more valuable than answers.

The 1–10 Question

Once you've learned about their current system:

“Just so I understand where you're at, on a scale of 1–10, how satisfied are you with the way you're handling urgent transportation right now?”

If they say 10: “That's great. What makes it a 10?” Learn.

If they say 7: “Got you. Why isn't it a 10?” That's where the conversation gets interesting.

Never tell them why they're wrong. Let them tell you.

Digging Deeper

When they identify a problem:

  • “How long has that been an issue?”
  • “What have you tried so far?”
  • “Why hasn't that completely solved it?”
  • “What happens when that occurs?”
  • “How does that affect your operation?”
  • “How important is it for you to fix that?”

Then: “On a scale of 1–10, how important is it?” If they say 6: “Why isn't it lower?”

Don't ask: “Why isn't it higher?”

Ask: “Why isn't it lower?”

They will sell themselves on the importance of solving their own problem.

Current State → Desired State

Ask: “Where are you now?” Understand it. Then: “Where would you like to be instead?” Then: “What's keeping you from getting there?”

Now you have the gap. That's what POTENT sells into.

Not a truck. Not a mile. Not a transportation service. A solution to a problem they already told you they have.

The Customer Is Not An Interruption

If the phone rings while you're doing something else — that's the business. Don't make the customer feel like they're bothering you. Answer with energy.

“POTENT Operations, this is [NAME]. How can I help you?”

Then listen. Don't immediately interrupt with “What's the pickup?” First understand what they need.

The biggest mistake in cold outreach is trying to sell immediately. A cold call is not where you close a deal. A cold call is where you earn the next conversation.

The POTENT First Contact Mindset

You are not interrupting. You are not begging for business. You are introducing a potential solution.

Businesses have problems every day. Your job is to find out if one of those problems exists.

People decide quickly if they want to continue listening.

Lead Temperature (Chapter 8)

The POTENT Lead Temperature System

Hot — Ready

Signs: has a current problem, needs a solution soon, asked about pricing, wants next steps, introduced decision makers.

Action: move quickly. Schedule. Quote. Close.

Warm — Potential

Signs: interested, has occasional problems, not urgent today, wants information.

Action: nurture. Follow up. Provide value. Stay visible.

Cold — Not Ready

Signs: no need, no interest, no problem, poor fit.

Action: do not waste energy. Stay professional. Move on.
